The campaign

Three eras, each with its own sky, its own hazards, its own boss.

Every stage is hand-laid — ground segments, one-way platforms, chasms, turrets and flying drones — and every stage ends the same way: the camera locks, the gate shuts behind you, and something very large wakes up.

Look and feel

Pixel-pure, but nothing is a box any more.

Every character is built from rounded caps, arcs, ellipses and bezier limbs instead of stacked rectangles — helmets, visors, shoulder pads, knees and boots all have real silhouettes. The same rig is reskinned per era, jittered in scale on every spawn, and roughly one enemy in eight arrives as a gold elite with nearly twice the health.

Readability got the same treatment: enemy bullets carry a dark rim, a glow and a motion trail; arrows at the screen edge point at enemies you cannot see; and the background dims while a boss is on screen.

Edge to edge

No page padding, no letterbox gaps — the canvas owns the whole viewport on a phone held sideways.

The logical viewport follows your window from 16:9 up to 21:9. Boss arenas keep a fixed 16:9 footprint and the camera centres them, so a wide screen sees the arena walls rather than empty void.

Arsenal

Five weapons. You lose the good one when you die.

Weapons drop from floating capsules and stay with you until you take a fatal hit — then it is back to the rifle. That single rule turns every pickup into a decision about how much further you are willing to push.

RIFLEDefault. Even cadence, clean damage, always with you.
SPREADThree pellets in a fan. Best at bad-breath range.
MACHINEFast, jittery, forgiving. Melts anything that stands still.
LASERSlow to fire, punches through three targets at once.
FLAMEShort reach, wide cloud, burns through a crowd.

Bosses

Three of them. Each one has a second phase.

Half health flips a switch: the Warden fires wider spreads and stomps shockwaves, the Glacier Wraith calls in drones, and the Eon Heart starts spinning its volleys faster than you can walk.

ERA I BOSS

THE WARDEN

A walking bunker with shoulder cannons and a ground shockwave you have to jump.

ERA III BOSS

THE EON HEART

It does not move. It does not have to. The spiral closes in from every angle.

Between them sits the GLACIER WRAITH — airborne, wing-flapping, dropping ice shards in a fan and calling in reinforcements when it drops below half health.

How it's built

No engine, no assets, no network.

Everything you see is drawn from rectangles and paths at runtime: the commando, the enemies, the bosses, the parallax skylines, the 5×7 pixel font on the HUD. Everything you hear is synthesised from oscillators and noise buffers in the Web Audio API — there is no .png, no .wav, no font file, no CDN.

SIMULATION

Fixed 60 Hz step

A fixed timestep with an accumulator, so physics behaves the same on a 60 Hz laptop and a 144 Hz monitor.

MOVEMENT

Coyote time & buffering

Six frames of coyote time and seven frames of jump buffering, so a jump pressed a hair early still counts.

AUDIO

Chiptune at runtime

Two-pulse leads, a triangle bass and a noise kit, sequenced ahead of time against the audio clock.

The shipped game is one file. The source is split into readable parts under src/ — core, audio, input, art, content, entities, game, render, main — and build.py concatenates them in order. Edit a part, rebuild, reload.
